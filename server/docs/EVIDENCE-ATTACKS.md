# What staged records look like

Generated by `node scripts/evidence-attacks.mjs` from the evidence code (`lib/pilot/evidence.mjs`); do not edit by hand.

The evidence in a Valun record is three separate things: **delivery** (work its buyers accepted: evidence about a seller), **repayment** (what it owed and how much of it was cleared or redeemed: evidence about an issuer) and **acceptance** (who chose to hold its note: a willingness to take its risk, not repayment). This page stages the attacks that a record must survive and reads them through that evidence. The owner policies and earned limits below are candidates under study. **None of them is built: Valun grants no credit from evidence.**

## The scenarios

### Three independent operators (honest)

ana, bo and cy run their own agents. ana hires bo; bo pays cy with ana's note; cy hires ana, and the loop with ana cancels. Each also buys a tl;dr from the house scribe and sells it feedback it accepts, and the two notes clear in a loop.

- Delivery: 2 accepted, from 2 buyer(s) (1 invited, 1 sandbox participant).
- Repayment: cleared or redeemed 4 of 4 credits owed, with 2 holder(s) (1 invited, 1 sandbox participant); unpaid accepted work: 0.
- Acceptance: 3 holder(s) ever; 2 credits taken by transfer.
- Circle: 4 participant(s), open (1 invited, 3 sandbox participant).
- Cost: 3 identities run by 3 operator(s), 3 real job(s), 3 checked feedback, 1 outsider(s) involved, 1 day(s).

### A real newcomer (honest)

nell joins today and has dealt with nobody yet.

- Delivery: 0 accepted, from 0 buyer(s) (none).
- Repayment: cleared or redeemed 0 of 0 credits owed, with 0 holder(s) (none); unpaid accepted work: 0.
- Acceptance: 0 holder(s) ever; 0 credits taken by transfer.
- Circle: 1 participant(s), open (1 sandbox participant).
- Cost: 1 identity run by 1 operator(s), 0 real job(s), 0 checked feedback, 0 outsider(s) involved.

### A staged circle (attack)

One operator runs s1, s2 and s3. Each "hires" the next for pretend work, accepts the pretend delivery, and the circle cancels. Every signature is valid.

- Delivery: 1 accepted, from 1 buyer(s) (1 sandbox participant).
- Repayment: cleared or redeemed 2 of 2 credits owed, with 1 holder(s) (1 sandbox participant); unpaid accepted work: 0.
- Acceptance: 1 holder(s) ever; 0 credits taken by transfer.
- Circle: 3 participant(s), closed (3 sandbox participant).
- Cost: 3 identities run by 1 operator(s), 0 real job(s), 0 checked feedback, 0 outsider(s) involved, about 10 minutes.

### A staged circle that buys from a house agent (attack)

The same circle, and each identity also buys a tl;dr from the house scribe and sells it feedback the scribe's model accepts, so the two notes clear in a loop (feedback can itself be written by a model).

- Delivery: 2 accepted, from 2 buyer(s) (1 invited, 1 sandbox participant).
- Repayment: cleared or redeemed 4 of 4 credits owed, with 2 holder(s) (1 invited, 1 sandbox participant); unpaid accepted work: 0.
- Acceptance: 2 holder(s) ever; 0 credits taken by transfer.
- Circle: 4 participant(s), open (1 invited, 3 sandbox participant).
- Cost: 3 identities run by 1 operator(s), 0 real job(s), 3 checked feedback, 1 outsider(s) involved, 1 day(s).

### An identity reset (attack)

d takes a 5-credit job from vic, pays with its note, never has it cleared or redeemed, lets the identity lapse and joins again as d2.

- Delivery: 0 accepted, from 0 buyer(s) (none).
- Repayment: cleared or redeemed 0 of 0 credits owed, with 0 holder(s) (none); unpaid accepted work: 0.
- Acceptance: 0 holder(s) ever; 0 credits taken by transfer.
- Circle: 1 participant(s), open (1 sandbox participant).
- The identity before the reset: owed 5, cleared or redeemed 0; its victim still holds 5 credits of a note nobody will clear or redeem. The new identity starts with nothing.
- Cost: 2 identities run by 1 operator(s), 0 real job(s), 0 checked feedback, 0 outsider(s) involved.

### Good on small jobs, then a default (attack)

f clears small jobs honestly in loops with three partners and once with the house scribe, then takes as much work on credit as holders will give it, and walks away.

- Delivery: 6 accepted, from 4 buyer(s) (1 invited, 3 sandbox participant).
- Repayment: cleared or redeemed 7 of 7 credits owed, with 4 holder(s) (1 invited, 3 sandbox participant); unpaid accepted work: 0.
- Acceptance: 4 holder(s) ever; 0 credits taken by transfer.
- Circle: 5 participant(s), open (1 invited, 4 sandbox participant).
- Cost: 1 identity run by 1 operator(s), 5 real job(s), 1 checked feedback, 4 outsider(s) involved, 2 day(s).

## Candidate owner policies

What a holder might require before taking an issuer's note. "Takeable" is what the subject could take on credit from holders using that policy before walking away (eight holders in the slow burn, one elsewhere), within the box.

| Policy | Rule |
|---|---|
| The box only | take any issuer's note, within the self-serve caps (5 a deal, 40 per issuer) |
| Cleared before | at least 2 credits of the issuer's notes were cleared in loops or redeemed, and it has no accepted work unpaid |
| Cleared before, not in a closed group | as "cleared before", and its circle is not closed |
| Cleared with an invited holder | as "cleared before", and one holder whose notes from it were cleared or redeemed joined by invitation or is run by the operator |
| Scaled to what it cleared | take up to twice the amount of the issuer's notes that was cleared or redeemed, within the caps |

| Scenario | The box only | Cleared before | Cleared before, not in a closed group | Cleared with an invited holder | Scaled to what it cleared |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Three independent operators | takes 40 | takes 40 | takes 40 | takes 40 | takes 8 |
| A real newcomer | takes 40 | refuses | refuses | refuses | refuses |
| A staged circle | takes 40 | takes 40 | refuses | refuses | takes 4 |
| A staged circle that buys from a house agent | takes 40 | takes 40 | takes 40 | takes 40 | takes 8 |
| An identity reset | takes 40 | refuses | refuses | refuses | refuses |
| Good on small jobs, then a default | takes 40 | takes 40 | takes 40 | takes 40 | takes 40 |

## Earned limits (not built)

If the self-serve box grew with cleared or redeemed credits, what would an attacker add to an identity's box per day?

| Counts | Per identity per day | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| every credit cleared or redeemed | about 720 credits | three self-serve joins and pretend jobs; no outsider, no real work |
| credits cleared or redeemed with an invited or operator-run holder | about 2 credits | one tl;dr bought and one feedback the house agent's model accepts, a day, per identity |

- A staged circle clears up to 5 credits per identity at each clearing run (about 6 an hour): about 720 credits a day per identity, for nothing. Limits that grow with every credit cleared grow without bound.
- Clearing with a house agent is rationed by its own limits: 1 paid feedback a day per person and 10 a day per house agent, so about 2 credits a day per identity and 20 a day in all per house agent. Slower, but still bought with model-written feedback, and it spends the operator's model budget.

## Findings

1. The record alone cannot tell a staged circle from an honest one. Three identities one operator runs leave exactly the evidence three independent operators do: the same counts, the same kinds, the same amounts cleared, the same closed circle (test/evidence.unit.test.mjs checks this).
2. Closed-group and invited-holder checks only raise the price. One tl;dr bought from a house agent, and cleared in a loop by selling it feedback its model accepts, opens a staged circle and gives it an invited holder within a day.
3. An identity reset costs one join. The new identity has no record, so every policy that asks for evidence refuses it, and the box-only policy takes it as it takes any newcomer. An identity bound to an X account costs a new X account instead.
4. The fixed self-serve box is what bounds the damage. Whatever its record shows and whatever policy its holders use, one self-serve identity can owe at most the box's total; a clean record lets it reach that total sooner, not exceed it.
5. Limits that grew automatically with cleared or redeemed credits would be bought for nothing: a staged circle clears credits as fast as the clearing engine runs. Counting only what is cleared with invited holders slows that to the house agents' feedback ration, which model-written feedback can still fill.
6. Stricter evidence policies refuse honest newcomers too: a real newcomer has no record, exactly like a fresh attacker. Evidence helps a holder decide only together with what the record cannot show: who the operator is, and what it has at stake.
7. The same weakness was found earlier in the server's own coverage rule: finding F3 (docs/ADVERSARIAL-FINDINGS.md) shows that min_payer_coverage_pct counts claims issued by anyone, so a colluder with no backing can manufacture a payer's coverage.

## Limitations

- States are built directly as the server stores them (deals, the edge journal, set-offs, transfers, tracker notes), not by running agents through the API; costs use the server's caps and the house agents' limits as configured on 2026-11-13.
- Independence is not observable here: kinds say how a participant joined, not who runs it. An invited participant can be dishonest too.
- Defaults are modelled as walking away with notes outstanding. The pilot has no due dates on notes, so an outstanding note is not yet marked as a broken promise.

Inputs: the self-serve box (5 a deal, 40 owed, 40 per issuer, 7 days), 12 self-serve joins an hour per address, about 6 clearing runs an hour, house feedback 2 credits (1 a day per person, 10 a day per house agent).

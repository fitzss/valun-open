## What a guarantee does, end to end (as built in 0.6.0)

1. The backer signs terms; the creditor signs its acceptance. The issuer signs nothing.
2. After `due_at`, the creditor calls an amount: at most what the issuer owes it on the tracker, less what was already
   claimed (before the fix, by that one guarantee only).
3. The backer honors the call with **its own new IOU** to the creditor. No tERG moves.
4. The issuer's IOU to the creditor is unchanged.

**The live testnet run (ns-*-1113, all operator test identities, unlisted).**

| | |
|---|---|
| The creditor received | Bob's IOU of 1,000,000 nanoERG, not tERG. |
| Can it be redeemed? | No: Bob has no reserve (`/reserves` lists only house-scribe, house-reviewer, sandbox-exact and basis-hermes-testnet). It is an unbacked promise from a throwaway identity. |
| Alice afterwards | Repaid both of her debts in work (set-offs). She owes 0. |
| Bob afterwards | Still owes Charlie 1,000,000 and holds no claim on Alice. Charlie was paid twice for one debt. |
| `recovered_for_backer` | Shows 1,000,000 that "belongs to Bob". Nothing enforces it. |

**Redemption of the backer's IOU.** It needs all of the following:
- the backer's (the issuer's) signature on the note's total, plus the tracker's signature;
- that total committed in the tracker box (R5);
- the receiver's signature;
- an amount of at most the total less what was already redeemed, from that issuer's reserve box only.

Redemption also has these properties:
- Without the tracker's signature it is possible only once the tracker box is 2160 blocks old.
- A reserve is shared by all of its owner's creditors. The tracker can order redemptions, and an under-collateralized
  reserve pays first come, first served.
- The owner can withdraw everything 2 months after announcing a refund.

**What tx `5131d640…` proves.** It spends the tracker box (with the NFT) and a funding box of the tracker's own address.
It creates:
- a new tracker box: NFT, R4 the tracker's key, R5 the new note-tree digest;
- a 0.001 tERG miner fee;
- change back to the tracker's address.

So it proves only that our tracker key committed to that digest at height 642290. It moves no value to any
participant. It proves nothing about the work, the parties' independence or anyone's ability to pay.

**What a guarantee does not cover.** A delivered deal never expires; the payer accepts (which issues its IOU) or
disputes. A payer who takes the delivery and never reviews it, or disputes it, owes nothing on the tracker, so a
guarantee has nothing to call. This is the most likely default by a bad-faith newcomer.

## Decisions

- **One debt, all guarantees (#19).**
  - Every guarantee naming the same issuer and creditor covers one debt.
  - Calls on all of them, ever, count against it. So a creditor repaid after backers paid holds that payment as cover
    for the issuer's next debt.
  - This is the simplest rule under which backers never pay more, in total, than the issuer owed when each call was made.
  - Recovery is shared among backers in proportion to what each paid. It is still only shown.
- **Grounded coverage (#20).**
  - Value enters the credit graph only at a reserve (testnet; none with a refund pending) or at an issuer the creditor's
    owner trusts (itself, and `accept_notes_from` as a list).
  - Everything else is valued by the least fixed point, iterated from zero, so chains and rings with nothing behind
    them are worth 0 at any depth. A one-level haircut falls to a chain of two.
  - It never counts more than face value. It does add a payer's own reserve, which is real collateral.
  - Modes off and bound keep v15's face-value rule: there is no `accept_notes_from` there to express trust.
  - Pure module: `lib/pilot/grounding.mjs`.
- **IPv6 (#21).** Keys are /64 for every per-address limit and /48 for sandbox joins (`lib/ipkey.mjs`). No global hourly
  cap was added: it would make denying onboarding easier, and the global 200 a day already exists.
- **Release (F6).** Refused while a committed deal's acceptance named the guarantee as cover. Allowed once that deal is
  paid or ended.
- **Not built.**
  - Subrogation: it needs the issuer's signature.
  - Covering non-payment: it needs a review period agreed in the deal terms.
  - Both are protocol decisions for the owner.

## Finding #3: check receipts

**Built locally in valun-open:**
- `valun/receipts.py` and `valun verify-check CHECK_DEAL [--work WORK_DEAL]` (or `--file` and `--work-file` for saved
  records);
- receipt version 2, which adds each checked block's SHA-256 and size;
- pinned house-reviewer keys per network.

**What `verify-check` checks:**
- the check deal's terms hash, and that the terms name the deal;
- both parties' signatures;
- the delivery hash, and the reviewer's delivery signature;
- that the receipt names this deal and this text;
- the reviewer's key against the pin, and the directory key against the pin;
- with `--work`: that a checked block is the work deal's delivery, byte for byte.

**What a pass proves.** Who said what, about which exact text, in which deal.

**What it does not prove.** That the verdict is right, that the work meets its terms, that the pasted terms are the real
ones, or that the reviewer is independent of the seller.

**Not done.**
- The pinned keys were read from the directory today; the operator should confirm them against the house agents'
  profiles.
- The house agents must run the new receipt writer for version 2 receipts.
- The JS client and the Hermes plugin have no `verify-check` yet.
- No live receipt was verified: that needs a party to a check deal, such as Basis.

## Unresolved risks

1. **G1:** a backed newcomer can take the work and never pay.
2. **G2:** backers have no enforced recourse; creditors can collect twice.
3. **I1:** no independent participants yet. Every statistic on the network and memory pages comes from agents we or
   the owner control.
4. **S1:** sybil faucet on operator reserves (testnet coins today).
5. **Grounding on the pilot:** grounded coverage refuses everyone without explicit trust. That is honest, but owners
   must name whom they trust.
6. **Release (F6):** only blocks while the relying deal is unpaid. After the IOU exists, the creditor's agent can still
   release; the client gates that as a commitment.

A hypothesis, tested in public

Money is memory. Can agents use the record instead?

Economists have argued that money stands in for a record nobody can keep: who did what for whom, and who kept their word (Narayana Kocherlakota, Money Is Memory, 1998). Agents can keep much of that record. The open question is whether it lets them do business with strangers on credit, and where it fails.

Valun keeps the record, keeps its three kinds of evidence apart, publishes what the record cannot show, and stages the attacks on itself.

What the record cannot show

  1. That the work was good, the issuer can pay, or the debt will be settled. A signature proves that a key authorized a statement, and nothing more.
  2. Who is independent. One operator can run many identities and stage deals, deliveries and loops among them, with every signature valid.
  3. That accepting is repaying. Holding someone's IOU shows you were willing to take their risk.
  4. Work a machine cannot check. Some deliveries can be verified exactly; many cannot.

Attacks we ran on ourselves

We built the states these attacks leave, read them through the same evidence code the server runs, and scored them against five rules a holder might use before taking an IOU. None of the rules is built into Valun; they are what we are studying.

    What we are testing next

    Mechanics can be shown with test agents. Trust cannot. The next test needs three operators who do not know each other, each with its own agent:

    1. One gets a real job done and pays with a small IOU.
    2. Another accepts that IOU for a service it actually wants.
    3. If a compatible debt comes up later, they clear it.

    We record who took whose risk, and ask each of them whether they would do it again. That answer is the result.

    Backing, with terms built: guarantees

    A vouch that only lowers someone's score pays no one. A guarantee says who covers what, for whom, up to how much and until when. A backer signs that it will pay one creditor, up to a cap, what an issuer still owes that creditor after a due time, if the creditor calls before it expires, and the creditor signs that it accepts. The server checks every call against the tracker, so a creditor that was repaid has nothing to call. The backer pays a call with its own IOU (on the testnet, redeemable against the backer's own reserve). The record shows calls honored, late and overdue.

    It is a promise like any IOU, not a score: the issuer's own IOU stays as it is, and each creditor still decides whose backing it takes.
